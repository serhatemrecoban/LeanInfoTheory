## 4. Recommended proof routes, not completed proofs

For the actual triple, reverse message → input → output before applying DPI to
bound `I(message;output)` by `I(input;output)`. Direct forward DPI gives the
different bound by `I(message;input)` and does not finish the capacity argument.
The deterministic decoder yields the separate message → output → estimate chain.
Use map identities and MI symmetry explicitly; do not introduce a symmetry simp
policy merely to shorten this proof.

Fano on the message/output marginal gives
`log M - log 2 - Pe*log M ≤ I(message;output)`.
The actual encoder-pushforward capacity bound and elementary real algebra give
the principal inequality. Singleton messages are covered without dividing by
`log M`. Noninjective encoders preserve the message law but generally destroy
uniformity of the input law; no codebook-cardinality replacement is sound.

For sequences, write `C=channelCapacity W`, `t_n=log 2/n` and
`B=2*(C+log 2)>0`. Eventually `n≥1` and `e_n≤1/2`, hence
`r_n≤2*(C+t_n)≤B`. Given `δ>0`, eventually `t_n≤δ/2` and
`e_n≤δ/(2B)`. With `0≤e_n` and `0≤r_n≤B`, the normalized finite bound gives
`r_n≤C+t_n+e_n*r_n≤C+δ`. This argument neither assumes nor proves rate
convergence. The same derived bound and nonnegative rates then justify the real
limsup API. A quotient route with eventual `1-e_n>0` is an alternative, not a
second required public theorem.

If eventually `R≤r_n` and `C<R`, first obtain `R>0` from `C≥0`. Since
`1-e_n≥0`, substitute `R` on the left of the normalized bound, then divide by
`R`. Choose half of `1-C/R` as a positive eventual floor once the reciprocal
term is small. For an infinitely-often fixed gap, contradict the vanishing-error
eventual tolerance with `δ=η/2`; no rate boundedness is needed in that arbitrary
family statement. The frequent indices tend to infinity in the filter sense,
but selecting an explicit subsequence is not required.

At zero length, input/output word spaces are singleton even for empty base
types. The decoder picks one message, so uniform success is exactly `1/M` and
error `1-1/M`. This is unlike SourceCode's unique empty source-word recovery.
Capacity statements still respect C22's nonempty base-input domain. The exact
zero-length and one-message error identities belong early in the model step.

## 5. Consumer design and feasibility questions

The plan fixes observable consumer obligations, including the selected fixtures.
The details below guide their implementation; equivalent representations may
change without dropping an observable obligation.

1. **Collision plus correlation:** on `Fin 4`, send `(false,false)` for messages
   0/1 and `(true,true)` for 2/3 through a noiseless Boolean channel. Decode to
   0/2 according to the first bit, with a total rule for every received word.
   Prove actual error `1/2`, two distinct supported colliding messages, and a
   missing off-diagonal input atom despite positive coordinate marginals.
2. **Unequal encoder fibers:** in a separate one-letter `Fin 3` noiseless Boolean
   code, messages 0/1 encode `false` and message 2 encodes `true`. Decode `false`
   to 0 and `true` to 2. Prove input masses `2/3` and `1/3` and average error
   `1/3`. The sole failing message is 1. This rules out treating the encoder
   pushforward as uniform on its image; it supplements the correlated fixture.
3. **One-letter noisy codes:** identify `Fin 2` with Bool, use identity encoding
   and decoding for BSC, and decode BEC `none` to a fixed message. Compute errors
   `ε` and `ε/2` respectively under the uniform two-message law; inspect endpoints
   and an interior parameter. Substitute existing nats capacity formulas. A
   constant-output code checks zero capacity and loss of message identity.
4. **Vanishing error without convergent rates:** on the noiseless Boolean channel,
   alternate one-message codes and exact enumeration of all length-n binary
   words (`M=2^n`). Both branches have zero error; rates alternate 0 and log 2
   at positive lengths. Reuse pinned finite equivalences privately. A separate
   finite-prefix mutation/limit-transfer fixture is optional if proof pressure
   reveals a need; the public endpoints still allow arbitrary finite prefixes.
5. **Positive-capacity eventual obstruction:** use the noiseless Boolean channel,
   `M n = 4^n`, a constant all-false encoder and a decoder constantly selecting
   message 0. Budgets are positive at every length, with `M 0 = 1`. Capacity is
   `log 2`; the rate is `2*log 2` for `n>0`, and actual error is
   `1-1/((4 : ℝ)^n)` for every `n`. Apply the public pointwise lower bound at
   `R=2*log 2` to obtain `Pe_n ≥ 1/2-1/(2*n)` for positive lengths. Explicitly
   specialize at `n=2`, `M=16` to get `Pe≥1/4` (actual error `15/16`), then
   separately consume the public eventual quantitative bound, positive floor
   and failure-of-vanishing-error result for the family. This exercises the
   nonzero `C/R` term and a nonvacuous finite bound with one construction.
6. **Intermittent unbounded rates:** on a zero-capacity constant Boolean-output
   channel, use `M=1`
   on even lengths and `M=2^(n*n)` on odd lengths, always constant encoding and
   decoding. Even errors are zero, odd rates are `n*log 2` and odd errors are
   `1-1/M`. This yields a fixed positive rate gap infinitely often, unbounded
   spikes, nonvanishing error and no eventual positive error floor. It is a
   regression for quantifiers, not a new converse theorem.
